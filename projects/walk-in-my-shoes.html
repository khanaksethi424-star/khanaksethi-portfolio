Walk In My Shoes

An empathy-driven mobile app letting users experience life through the perspectives of marginalized individuals — created for the Taiwan International Student Design Competition, theme "Diversity."

Overview

Walk In My Shoes is an interactive mobile app that puts users inside narrative-driven scenarios shaped by identities different from their own — designed as an entry for the Taiwan International Student Design Competition under the theme "Diversity."

The problem

People struggle to understand lives shaped by different identities, which feeds stereotypes and exclusion. Existing empathy-building tools tend to be either too shallow to create real understanding, or too resource-heavy — think enterprise VR training — to reach a broad, everyday audience.

Process

Discover

I analyzed existing empathy-building and diversity-training apps and found a clear gap: there wasn't an accessible, narrative-driven, interactive mobile experience aimed at everyday users rather than corporate training rooms.

Empathize

I grounded the personas in secondary research from the WHO, UN DESA, Pew Research Center, and NIH, covering disability and accessibility, religious minority discrimination, and body image and PCOS — then built empathy maps and journey maps from that research to keep the stories authentic rather than assumed.

Define

The core problem was framed around educating young adults and students, with success measured by emotional connection, reflection, and awareness — including an in-app score reflecting accessibility, agency, and inclusivity. The target audience spanned students, educators, and organizations.

Ideate

I ran Crazy 8's ideation to explore directions fast, landing on a visual identity built around deep purple and maroon for empathy and seriousness, warm neutrals for approachability, and teal/pink highlights for decision points and gamification. The core feature became interactive narratives paired with gamification — badges and progress tracked across three metrics: Self-Advocacy, Safety, and Emotional Well-Being.

Prototype

The final prototype uses immersive storytelling screens, color-coded choice buttons for decision points, a feedback scorecard, and character profiles with real backstories grounded in the research.

Solution

A narrative-first mobile experience where users make choices as a character facing real, research-grounded challenges — closing with a reflective scorecard rather than a simple "you win" ending, keeping the focus on understanding rather than gamified competition.
