Walk In My Shoes

An empathy-driven mobile app letting users experience life through the perspectives of marginalized individuals — created for the Taiwan International Student Design Competition, theme "Diversity."

Overview

Walk In My Shoes is an interactive mobile app that puts users inside narrative-driven scenarios shaped by identities different from their own — designed as an entry for the Taiwan International Student Design Competition under the theme "Diversity."

The problem

People struggle to understand lives shaped by different identities, which feeds stereotypes and exclusion. Existing empathy-building tools tend to be either too shallow to create real understanding, or too resource-heavy — think enterprise VR training — to reach a broad, everyday audience.

Process

The gap in existing empathy tools

Diversity-training and empathy-building apps tend to fall into two camps: too shallow to create real understanding, or too resource-heavy — think enterprise VR — to reach an everyday audience. That gap became the brief: an accessible, narrative-driven, interactive mobile experience, not a corporate training tool.

Grounding the stories in real research

Rather than inventing scenarios, I built personas from secondary research — the WHO, UN DESA, Pew Research Center, and NIH — covering disability and accessibility, religious minority discrimination, and body image and PCOS. Empathy maps and journey maps came out of that research, so the stories stayed authentic rather than assumed.

Defining what success looks like

The goal was educating young adults and students, with success measured by emotional connection, reflection, and awareness — including an in-app score across accessibility, agency, and inclusivity. The intended audience spanned students, educators, and organizations, not just individual users.

From Crazy 8's to a narrative system

Crazy 8's ideation moved things fast, landing on a visual identity of deep purple and maroon for empathy and seriousness, warm neutrals for approachability, and teal and pink highlights for decision points and gamification. The core mechanic became interactive narratives layered with gamification — badges and progress tracked across three metrics: Self-Advocacy, Safety, and Emotional Well-Being.

Building the story, not just the screens

The final prototype leans on immersive storytelling screens, color-coded choice buttons at decision points, a feedback scorecard, and character profiles with full backstories grounded in the research — treating narrative structure as a design material, not just content dropped into a template.

Solution

A narrative-first mobile experience where users make choices as a character facing real, research-grounded challenges — closing with a reflective scorecard rather than a simple "you win" ending, keeping the focus on understanding rather than gamified competition.
