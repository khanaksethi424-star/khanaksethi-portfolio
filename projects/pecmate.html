UI/UX · Academic project · 5th semester

PECmate

A student-only carpooling concept for Punjab Engineering College, designed around verified community, recurring routes and the everyday friction of getting to campus.

RoleUI/UX Designer
ContextB.Des design project · PEC
ToolsFigma · visual exploration
TeamAcademic team project
01 / THE CONTEXT

Campus mobility was the starting point — trust became the product question.

The project began with everyday problems around commuting to PEC: private vehicles, parking pressure, fuel costs, traffic and the lack of a centralized student-only way to coordinate rides. The design brief was to explore a safer, more relevant alternative for the campus community.

RESEARCH SNAPSHOT
4,000+PEC students
8–15 kmtypical commute distance used in the project
₹150–₹250estimated daily fuel cost per student

Project research estimates from the semester study; figures are used here as design context rather than validated live campus statistics.

02 / WHAT THE RESEARCH CHANGED

Students did not just need a cheaper ride.

The survey highlighted reduced travel cost as a major motivation, but also surfaced concerns around schedule mismatch, pickup/drop-off convenience and sharing rides with unfamiliar people.

That shifted the concept toward a closed PEC community, visible ride context and flexible coordination — rather than simply recreating a public ridesharing marketplace.

03 / POSITIONING

Build for one campus, not everyone.

The project compared PECmate with general-purpose ridesharing and public transport. The differentiator was not on-demand scale; it was relevance to a known student community, shared routes and lower shared travel cost.

04 / MANUAL VISUAL EXPLORATION

The identity started on paper before it became a product mark.

Five early logo directions explored different ways to combine the car, route and PEC identity. The exploration was then refined through variations in colour, form and wordmark treatment before arriving at the final mark.

REFINEMENT

After the initial directions, the selected car + route idea was explored through multiple colour and lockup variations.

FINAL MARK
05 / DEFINING THE EXPERIENCE

Two sides of the same commute.

The flow had to support both sides of the exchange: a student looking for a ride and a student offering one. The wider project flow covers verification, profile setup, ride search, ride creation, scheduling, ride management and messaging.

06 / JOURNEY MAP

Designing around the moments where trust can break.

The journey map surfaced skepticism during discovery, uncertainty during onboarding and coordination risks around delays, no-shows and pickup points. These became reasons to make verification, ride context and coordination visible in the interface.

07 / INTERFACE DESIGN

Turn the service logic into screens students can act on quickly.

The final interface explores the core journey from account creation to offering a ride, setting stop prices, waiting for a match, meeting the driver and handling cancellation.

08 / REFLECTION

A campus constraint became the strongest product decision.

PECmate is intentionally scoped to a specific community. Working within one campus allowed trust, recurring routes, familiar pickup points and student verification to become part of the product logic rather than add-on features.

Portfolio takeawayA narrower problem can produce a clearer product when the constraint becomes part of the UX.
