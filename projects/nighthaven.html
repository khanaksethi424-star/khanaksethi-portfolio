Product Design

Nighthaven

A sleeping bag designed to give warmth, safety, and dignity to pavement dwellers facing life-threatening cold — balancing affordability, portability, and real insulation.

Role[Add role]
Duration[Add duration]
ToolsFigma, physical prototyping
Team[Add team]

Overview

Nighthaven is a product designed for one of the starkest gaps in urban design: there is no affordable, dignified way for people sleeping on pavements to survive severe cold. Open Government Data (India) recorded 10,933 deaths from cold and exposure between 2001 and 2014 — roughly 781 people every year.

The problem

There's a critical, life-threatening need for an affordable, durable, weatherproof, insulating sleeping bag for people experiencing homelessness — one that treats warmth and dignity as equally non-negotiable, not warmth alone.

Process

Discover

The scale of the problem came from Open Government Data — cold exposure isn't a rare tragedy, it's a recurring, largely invisible cause of death that design has mostly ignored.

Empathize

I built personas and an empathy map, and spoke with NGO workers in the field about portability, moisture resistance, and maintenance. Just as important as warmth: users consistently valued dignity and safety as much as being warm.

Define

Design goals came out of that research directly — affordability for mass distribution, real weatherproofing, high insulation without excess weight, durability and portability, modularity, and a gender-neutral, dignified aesthetic rather than something that reads as charity.

Ideate

I explored materials — waterproof ripstop nylon with a PU coating, polyfill versus black cotton versus Thinsulate versus Climashield insulation, microfleece lining, and a Mylar reflective base — alongside form concepts including a mobility-enhanced design, mummy-style, and quilted/flap/coffin-style variants.

Prototype

The final prototype used black cotton insulation for cost-effectiveness, a single side zipper, fewer seams, an integrated hood, and a compact roll-up form. I pilot tested it with 10 individuals in winter shelters.

Solution

A single-zipper, low-seam sleeping bag with an integrated hood and compact roll-up design. Two material versions were costed out: a polyfill version at approximately ₹1,500, and a black cotton version at approximately ₹700 — making the more affordable option genuinely viable for mass distribution.

Outcome & reflection

Pilot testing with 10 individuals in winter shelters surfaced clear, actionable feedback: it was warmer than blankets and easy to carry, but users wanted storage compartments. I iterated the design to add pockets and simplify the hood in response. This was the project where field feedback most directly reshaped the final design — a reminder that even a strong initial concept usually isn't the final answer.
