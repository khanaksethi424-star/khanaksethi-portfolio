UI/UX

Fruit Cider

A visually engaging, modern landing page for a fruit cider brand, using a 3D carousel to put Pear, Apple, and Exotic flavors center stage.

Duration[Add duration]
ToolsFigma

Overview

Most fruit cider brands rely on static banners with minimal interaction, missing an opportunity to make flavor itself feel exciting online. This project reimagines the landing page as something you'd actually want to play with.

The problem

Fruit cider brands struggle to create engaging, interactive digital presences for younger audiences — most competitor sites use static banners with little to no interaction, so the product never really comes to life on screen.

Process

Reading the competition

Market research pointed toward bold, playful, immersive UI as a rising trend for Gen Z and Millennial audiences, while competitor benchmarking confirmed most brands were still relying on static banners.

Who this is really for

I built a persona and user journey grounded in Belonging and Esteem from Maslow's hierarchy — for this audience, trendy products carry a social and self-image component, not just a taste preference.

The brief in one question

How might we design a playful, bold, interactive slider while keeping the product itself central? Goals included a strong flavor identity, a 3D carousel effect, and fruit cut-outs paired with expressive typography for freshness and energy.

Finding the right feel

I explored flavor discovery patterns, the balance between minimalism and playfulness, and how to keep the product as the visual hero — sketching and wireframing several slider layouts, and building moodboards around playful fruit imagery, bold type, and a pastel palette.

Building the carousel

The final high-fidelity prototype is a 3D Carousel Slider in Figma — Pear in green, Apple in red, Exotic in purple — with oversized overlapping typography, floating 3D fruit elements, and pastel backgrounds throughout.

Solution

A landing page built around a rotating 3D carousel, where switching flavors is itself the primary interaction — turning a normally passive scroll-through into something that feels closer to flipping through a flavor menu by hand.
